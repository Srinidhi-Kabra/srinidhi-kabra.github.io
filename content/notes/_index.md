I would like to take a moment to describe the underlying philosophy and motivation for the notes presented here. It is often the case that many mathematics courses sacrifice motivation and historical development of the subject in favor for standard exposition in the Bourbaki style - definition followed by theorem and proof - but the motivation behind many definitions introduced remain unclear, crucial ideas of the proof aren't emphasised. Technical mastery is developed in the form of rigorous problem solving, but seldom any attention is paid to the conceptual nuances shaping the subject. I like to view mathematics not as the study of certain objects but as a story with certain goals in mind - these objects then become the protagonists of our story. I have tried to integrate this personal view of the subject in my notes. Content has been organised in a manner that clarifies both historical motivations while motivating many definitions through certain observations and examples; without compromising on rigor. Put differently, as a person who values not just mastery of technicalities but understanding the manner of thinking which shapes mathematics; I have chosen a pedagogical route which would've made the subject more appealing to me, and I hope to anyone else reading it as well. Lastly, I do not claim to be an expert in any of these topics. Regardless, I would like to share some content that provided me with a new perspective of appreciating mathematical beauty. 


The notes are relatively self-contained, with relevant prerequisites being mentioned. An extensive bibliography is included. It is only recently that I have started digitising my notes as such many are incomplete. I hope to finish them as time permits, after which they will be displayed on this page.
